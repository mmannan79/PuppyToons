# Narration and Audio Guide

## Narrator

Use the same stable voice anchor throughout each episode:

“Friendly adult female narrator, warm and clear, medium-slow pace, natural General American English, bright but not shrill, smiling delivery, careful pronunciation, consistent tone and volume.”

A different voice may be used for a localized edition, but do not mix languages or accents randomly within one episode.

## Pacing

- Speak clearly and leave space between phrases.
- Read answer options at an even pace.
- Do not overlap the question with music stings or character sounds.
- Leave 8–12 seconds for thinking. A short cue such as “What do you think? Take your time!” may be spoken at the start of the gap, followed by quiet music or a gentle visual countdown.
- Never let the narrator, subtitles, color cues or music reveal the answer before the reveal moment.
- Read the script aloud and time it before generating the final voice track.

## Azula reactions

Use brief, repeatable sounds or lines such as “Woof-derful!”, “Let’s sniff out the answer!” or a happy bark. Avoid making the puppy bark over important words. Keep reactions gentle and not startling.

## Sound design

- Background music: light ukulele, soft piano, marimba or gentle xylophone; cheerful but not frantic.
- SFX: soft paw steps, a quiet sparkle, a gentle pop for choices, a warm chime for the reveal, soft celebratory flourish.
- Use licensed, public-domain or original audio. Keep records of source and license.
- Avoid loud sudden impacts, alarm sounds, harsh ticking, or repeated sound effects that may overwhelm the child.

## Mixing guidelines

- Narration is always the clearest layer.
- Keep music comfortably below speech and duck it further during questions and explanations.
- Use consistent loudness between scenes. Remove clicks, clipping, abrupt music changes and long accidental silences.
- Use separate audio tracks when possible. This lets the editor create the thinking pause and correct timing without regenerating the animation.

## Voice and rights

Do not clone a real person's voice without appropriate authorization. AI voice tools can drift in tone and pronunciation; listen to every line. Fix names, answer words and any mispronunciation manually before publishing.
