# Five-Question Episode Template (6–8 Minutes)

## Runtime map

Default target: about **6:45**. Time each section after narration and editing; the estimates below are flexible.

| Time | Segment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | Intro | Welcome, introduce the topic and explain how to play |
| 0:25–1:25 | Question 1 | Setup, question, options, thinking pause, reveal, explanation, payoff |
| 1:25–2:25 | Question 2 | Same rhythm, new visual environment or prop |
| 2:25–3:25 | Question 3 | Same rhythm, add one small clue |
| 3:25–4:25 | Question 4 | Same rhythm, encourage independent guessing |
| 4:25–5:25 | Question 5 | Final puzzle and celebration |
| 5:25–6:20 | Recap | Repeat the five answers briskly and invite viewers to answer along |
| 6:20–6:45 | Outro | Celebrate learning and say goodbye |

## Per-question timing template

- 0–10 s: Azula discovers a simple problem or mystery.
- 10–18 s: Narrator asks the question once, clearly.
- 18–28 s: Show 3–4 answer options. Read them once at a calm pace.
- 28–38 s: **Thinking pause, 8–12 seconds.** Invite the child to say the answer aloud. Do not say, show or subtitle the answer early.
- 38–45 s: Reveal the correct option visually and verbally.
- 45–55 s: Explain why in one or two simple sentences.
- 55–60 s: Azula reacts and transition to the next mini-adventure.

Timing is approximate. If the spoken explanation needs more room, shorten the setup or transition rather than rushing the question or thinking pause.

## Question card design

- 16:9 landscape, consistent layout across the episode.
- One short question at the top.
- Three or four large option cards with clear images and short labels.
- Avoid small type, busy backgrounds, low contrast and decorative elements that compete with options.
- Use a subtle countdown indicator during the thinking pause, not a stressful timer.
- Add final typography in the editor if generated text is misspelled, distorted or inconsistent.

## Script style

- Use short sentences and concrete words.
- Repeat the key noun when needed; avoid pronouns that make the answer ambiguous.
- Ask the viewer to think, but never pressure them to comment or disclose personal information.
- Encourage effort without saying a child is “bad” or “wrong.”
- Use inclusive phrases such as “Let’s find out!” and “Good thinking!”
