# PuppyToons Playbook v2

**Channel:** [PuppyToons-AI on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@PuppyToons-AI)
**Series:** Azula's Little Learning Quiz!
**Audience:** Children ages 3–8, watching with family
**Format:** 16:9 landscape, 1920×1080

This playbook replaces v1. It is built from three inputs: the quiz workflow in [Kids Quiz Videos With AI](https://theradiotv.net/kids-quiz-videos-with-ai/), the original [PuppyToons repository](https://github.com/mmannan79/PuppyToons), and Mo's sample quiz clip and Baker Family character sheet, both supplied for this rebuild.

## The decision in one page

V1 asked for five questions stretched into a 6–8 minute episode, with Azula acting out a mini-adventure for every question. That is why the first VideoExpress clips missed the expectation: many story clips per question, slow pacing, lots of chances for Azula to change shape, and very little time actually spent quizzing.

V2 flips the ratio:

- **The quiz card is the episode.** One question = one full-screen quiz card, exactly like the sample clip.
- **More questions, less stretching.** A full episode has 20 questions, not 5 stretched questions.
- **The Baker Family lives between the quizzes.** Short, warm Azula-and-family scenes sit between rounds of five questions. They give the video its family-friendly heart without slowing down the quiz.
- **The reveal is made in the editor, not by AI.** AI makes the card and the gentle motion. You add the thinking pause, the highlight, and the check mark, so the answer never leaks early and the spelling is always right.
- **Work in batches of five.** Generate, check, and animate five cards at a time — the batch size VideoExpress handles well, at up to 10 seconds per clip.

If you only remember one line: **quiz cards do the teaching, family scenes do the feeling, the editor does the reveal.**

## What changed from v1 to v2

| Area | V1 | V2 |
|---|---|---|
| Questions per episode | 5 | 20 (4 rounds of 5) |
| Runtime target | 6–8 minutes | 9–11 minutes |
| Per-question build | 7 story shots | 1 quiz card + editor reveal |
| Azula's role | Acts out every question | Hosts intro, family scenes, reactions |
| Family cast | Optional, rarely used | Scheduled between every round |
| Thinking pause | 8–12 sec inside story clips | 6–8 sec on a held quiz card |
| Answer reveal | Generated by AI | Added in the editor, always after the pause |
| On-screen text | Generated by AI | Editor-added preferred; AI text must pass a spelling gate |
| Generation batch | Whole episode planned at once | Batches of 5 cards, checked before animating |

Why 20 questions and not the 40–50 suggested in the source guide? Forty-plus cards is a heavy generation and checking load for one creator, and it leaves no room for the family scenes that make PuppyToons different from a generic quiz channel. Twenty cards plus family scenes lands at the same 9–11 minute watch time with a clear identity. Scale to 30 later only if the 20-question workflow is running smoothly.

## The quiz card — anatomy from the sample clip

The sample clip Mo supplied is the template. It is 10 seconds, 1920×1080, and it works for a clear reason: a child can read the whole frame in one look.

- **Background:** one themed scene fills the whole frame — a farm, a jungle, a beach. It sets the topic before a word is read.
- **Top:** the question in large, bold, rounded letters, white with a dark outline, readable on a TV across a room. Maximum 12 words.
- **Middle:** four answer cards in a 2 × 2 grid. Each card is a cream, rounded rectangle with the picture on the left and a short label on the right: `A:`, `B:`, `C:`, `D:`.
- **Labels:** two or three words, with a friendly adjective — "Little Piggy," not just "Pig."
- **Motion:** the card itself does not move. The animals inside the cards blink, smile, and shift slightly. Stars twinkle, butterflies drift, light stays soft. The camera is locked.
- **Who is not in the card:** Azula. The quiz card is clean, with no host in the corner competing with the four choices. Azula appears before and after the round, not inside the card.

Memorize that frame. Every card in every episode should look like it belongs next to it.

## Episode structure

A full episode is four rounds of five questions, with a family scene at every join.

| Time (target) | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Intro with the family | Azula and the Bakers welcome viewers and name the topic |
| 0:30–2:40 | Round 1 — Questions 1–5 | Five quiz cards, about 26 seconds each in the edit |
| 2:40–3:10 | Family scene 1 | Azula + one family member, a small warm moment on the topic |
| 3:10–5:20 | Round 2 — Questions 6–10 | Five quiz cards, new sub-theme or setting |
| 5:20–5:50 | Family scene 2 | A different family pairing, gentle physical comedy |
| 5:50–8:00 | Round 3 — Questions 11–15 | Five quiz cards |
| 8:00–8:30 | Family scene 3 | Azula helps, sniffs out, or fetches something for the family |
| 8:30–10:00 | Round 4 — Questions 16–20 | Final five, slightly livelier pacing |
| 10:00–10:40 | Recap | The 20 answers, briskly, in order, with Azula reacting |
| 10:40–11:00 | Outro with the family | Thank viewers, celebrate effort, say goodbye |

Timings are targets to check against the assembled edit, not promises. If the edit runs long, trim family scenes and recap first — never cut the thinking pauses.

### How one question plays in the edit (about 26 seconds)

1. **Question (0–6 sec):** the animated card appears. The narrator reads the question once, clearly.
2. **Choices (6–12 sec):** the narrator reads the four labels in order, A to D, at an even pace.
3. **Thinking (12–19 sec):** hold the same card. No narration except an early, gentle "What do you think? Take your time!" Soft ticking or quiet music only. Nothing highlights, zooms, or points at the answer.
4. **Reveal (19–23 sec):** in the editor, add a green check and a soft glow to the correct card only, with a warm chime. The narrator says the answer with enthusiasm.
5. **One-line fact (23–26 sec):** one simple, true sentence about the answer — then cut straight to the next card.

### The pilot exception

Do not build all 20 questions first. Build **one round of five** as a pilot, assemble it with one family scene, watch it end to end, and fix the system before scaling. The worked pilot is at the end of this playbook.

## The Baker Family — character bible from the sheet

All descriptions below come from Mo's supplied character sheet, titled "The Baker Family." Use the sheet itself as the reference image in generation whenever the tool allows a reference. Never invent new outfits, hairstyles, or accessories for these characters.

### Azula — host

- Tiny, fluffy golden-brown Golden Retriever puppy, sitting pose at the center of the sheet.
- Floppy ears, big expressive brown eyes, small black nose, rounded puppy proportions.
- Teal-blue collar with a small tag. The collar never changes color.
- Personality: curious, affectionate, gently silly, proud when viewers guess well.
- Movement: bouncy small steps, tail wag, head tilt, soft blink, one paw lift. Calm during questions.

**Continuity lock — paste into every prompt that shows Azula:**

> Azula matches the approved reference exactly: one tiny fluffy golden-brown Golden Retriever puppy, floppy ears, expressive brown eyes, small black nose, puppy proportions, same teal-blue collar. Exactly one Azula. No duplication, morphing, extra limbs, or collar change.

### Mom — Sarah

- Brown hair in a bun, beige turtleneck sweater, dark jeans.
- Warm, patient, often laughing. On the sheet she holds a mug.
- Note for generation: do not ask AI to render text on the mug or clothing. Text on props is where AI spelling fails first — use a plain mug.

### Dad — David

- Brown hair, short beard, brown plaid shirt, khaki pants.
- Warm and playful. On the sheet he carries Leo in a dark blue baby carrier.
- The carrier appears only in scenes with Leo, and only when the prompt says so.

### Sister — Maya, age 4

- Brown hair in a side ponytail, purple cape, rainbow-striped shirt, dark patterned pants, yellow rain boots.
- Energetic and proud; on the sheet she holds up her own drawing.
- Maya is the family member most likely to guess out loud alongside the viewer.

### Baby brother — Leo, 9 months

- Short light-brown hair, blue-and-white striped onesie.
- Shown on a colorful play mat with toy blocks. He laughs, claps, and crawls — he never talks and never answers questions.
- Keep Leo in family scenes only, always with an adult or Maya nearby.

### Style lock for all family scenes

Polished, friendly 3D family-film animation. Rounded shapes, soft realistic fur on Azula, gentle warm lighting, simple uncluttered backgrounds, bright but not overwhelming color. No copyrighted characters, costumes, or logos. No flashing or strobe effects.

## Family scenes between the quizzes

Family scenes are the PuppyToons signature. A generic quiz channel has only cards; PuppyToons has a family viewers come back for.

### Rules

- **Length:** 20–30 seconds each, made from 2–3 clips of up to 10 seconds.
- **Cast:** Azula plus one or two family members. Rotate the pairing each scene. Leo only appears with an adult.
- **One job per scene:** set up the next round's theme, react to the round just played, or land one gentle joke. Never introduce a new quiz question inside a family scene.
- **One action per clip:** Azula sniffs a basket, Maya holds up a drawing, Dad lifts a picnic blanket. If a clip needs two actions, split it into two clips.
- **Dialogue:** short and sparse. One or two lines per scene, in the same narrator or character voice used across the episode. Most of the warmth should read from faces and action, not talking.
- **Ending beat:** every family scene ends with a clear handoff line or gesture toward the next round — "Ready for more? Let's go!"

### Scene ideas by pairing

| Pairing | Reliable beats |
|---|---|
| Azula + Maya | Maya draws the round's topic; Azula tilts her head at the drawing; they high-five / paw-five |
| Azula + Sarah | Baking, garden, or storybook moments; Azula "helps" by fetching one safe item |
| Azula + David + Leo | Picnic or play-mat scenes; Leo laughs at Azula's gentle hop; Azula settles and watches kindly |
| Whole family | Intro and outro only — the full cast is expensive to keep consistent, so save it for the bookends |

### What family scenes must never do

- Reveal or hint at an upcoming answer in a way that spoils the next round's first question.
- Show unsafe behavior a child might copy, frightening moments, or anyone being shamed for a wrong guess.
- Add new characters, pets, outfits, or props that were not in the plan for that episode.

## Production workflow in VideoExpress

Mo's working setup: VideoExpress.ai, All Access plan, clips generated up to five at a time, each clip up to 10 seconds, each taking roughly 30–60 seconds to render. This workflow is built around those numbers.

### Step 1 — Write the episode package first

Use Master Prompt 1 (below) to generate questions in batches of five, one batch per round. For each question you need: the question (12 words or fewer), four options, the one correct answer, a one-line fact, the themed background, and a narration line. Do not generate any images until all four rounds are written and fact-checked.

### Step 2 — Make the card images, in a batch of five

Use Master Prompt 2 for each card. Preferred method: generate the card with the four animal or object pictures and **clean, empty label areas**, then add the question and labels as text in the editor. Editor text is spelled by you, so it is spelled right.

If you generate text inside the AI image instead, that card must pass the spelling gate in Step 3 before it is animated. No exceptions.

### Step 3 — Gate: check every card before animating

Read each card out loud, letter by letter. Check the gate list in the quality section below. A failed card is regenerated as an image — that is cheap. Animating a failed card wastes a full render.

### Step 4 — Animate the batch of five

Animate each approved card with Master Prompt 3. Send up to five at a time, 10 seconds each. The motion is deliberately boring from a camera point of view — locked frame, living pictures — because the card layout must survive animation untouched.

### Step 5 — Make the family scenes

Generate the intro, the three between-round scenes, and the outro with Master Prompt 4, using the Baker Family sheet as reference. Two or three clips per scene, one action each. Check Azula and the cast against the sheet before accepting any clip.

### Step 6 — Record narration separately

Generate or record the full narration as its own audio, using the voice anchor in the audio section. Separate audio is what lets you place the thinking pause exactly, instead of accepting wherever a generated clip happens to pause.

### Step 7 — Assemble

Build the timeline in episode order: intro, round, family scene, round, and so on. For each question, lay the 10-second animated card in, then extend the thinking section by holding the card's last frame or looping its subtle motion. Add the reveal highlight, check mark, and chime in the editor at the reveal moment only. Add music underneath at a level that never competes with the narrator.

### Step 8 — Gate: watch the whole export

Watch the finished file from start to finish, with sound on, before uploading. Use the final checklist below. If a section fails, fix that section only — do not regenerate approved work.

## Master prompts

These prompts are written for PuppyToons v2. Copy them, fill the brackets, and keep the output format exactly — consistency across batches is what makes 20 separate cards feel like one episode.

### Prompt 1 — Quiz batch generator (five questions at a time)

> You are the PuppyToons quiz writer for children ages 3–8. Topic: [TOPIC]. Language: simple English only. Round number: [1–4].
>
> Write exactly five new questions that have never appeared in this episode. Each question is 12 words or fewer, concrete, kind, and answerable from the picture choices. Give four options labeled A–D, parallel in length, with exactly one clearly correct answer and three friendly, plausible, child-safe wrong answers. No trick questions.
>
> For each question, output in this order: question; options A–D; correct answer; one true, simple fact sentence about the answer (with the kind of source that supports it, such as a zoo or natural-history reference); themed background in five words or fewer; narrator line reading the question and options.
>
> Do not reveal the answer inside the narrator line. Keep every option's picture description to three words so it can be drawn clearly. When I type NEXT, give five completely new questions on the same topic and round rules.

### Prompt 2 — Quiz card image

> Premium 3D cartoon quiz card for a children's learning video, 16:9 landscape. Background: [THEMED SCENE, e.g., sunny farm with red barns, green grass, and flowers] filling the whole frame, soft light, bright and cheerful.
>
> Layout: clean space at the top for one large question line, added later. In the center, four large cream rounded rectangles in a 2 × 2 grid, evenly spaced. Top-left card: [OPTION A PICTURE]. Top-right card: [OPTION B PICTURE]. Bottom-left card: [OPTION C PICTURE]. Bottom-right card: [OPTION D PICTURE]. Each picture is a single cute 3D cartoon subject, big in its card, facing forward, easy to recognize at a glance. Leave a clean empty label strip on the right side of each card for text added later.
>
> Small stars, sparkles, and butterflies around the edges only. No text, no letters, no words, no logos, no watermarks, no copyrighted characters, nothing scary. Every subject stays fully inside its own card.

Why no text in the image: editor-added text cannot be misspelled, and the same card can be reused if a label needs fixing. If a tool cannot leave clean label space, generate with text and apply the spelling gate twice as strictly.

### Prompt 3 — Animate the quiz card (10 seconds)

> Animate this quiz card as one locked, continuous shot, 10 seconds, 16:9. The camera does not move. The layout does not change. The four cards stay in their exact positions and nothing leaves, enters, overlaps, or swaps places.
>
> Bring the pictures gently to life: each subject blinks, smiles, and moves a little in place — an ear twitch, a soft bob, a happy wiggle. Stars twinkle faintly, one butterfly drifts slowly at the edge, light stays soft and even.
>
> No highlight, glow, zoom, circle, arrow, or check mark on any card. No new objects, no morphing faces, no changing colors, no text appearing or moving. Calm, smooth, premium children's animation.

### Prompt 4 — Family scene (image, then motion)

Image:

> Premium 3D family-film animation still, 16:9 landscape, [SETTING, e.g., sunny Baker family garden]. [CAST, e.g., Azula and Maya] matching the approved Baker Family reference sheet exactly — same faces, hair, outfits, colors, and proportions. [STARTING POSE, one clear pose]. Warm soft light, simple uncluttered background, clean space, no text, no logos, no extra people, no duplicate characters.
>
> [Paste the Azula continuity lock, and the relevant locks for any family member in the shot.]

Motion:

> Animate the supplied still as one continuous shot, up to 10 seconds, preserving every character's face, outfit, and proportions. One action only: [ONE ACTION, e.g., Azula lifts one paw toward Maya's drawing]. One gentle background motion: [e.g., leaves sway softly]. Camera: locked medium-wide, or one very slow push-in. No new characters or props, no morphing, no costume changes, no sudden cuts, no camera shake. End on a calm, stable frame that can be held.

### Prompt 5 — Reveal (done in the editor — there is no AI prompt)

The reveal is an editing step, not a generation step:

1. Duplicate the card frame at the reveal moment.
2. Add a soft green glow and a large check mark to the correct card only.
3. Add a gentle scale-up of the correct card — about 5% — so it feels like it pops forward.
4. Play one warm chime, then let the narrator name the answer.
5. Leave the other three cards visible and unmarked. Children learn by seeing the full set one more time.

## Narration and audio

**Voice anchor — use the same wording everywhere a voice is set:**

> Friendly adult female narrator, warm and clear, medium-slow pace, natural General American English, bright but not shrill, smiling delivery, careful pronunciation, consistent tone and volume in every scene.

- One language per video. English and another language are separate editions, never mixed inside one episode.
- Read the question once, then the four options once, in order. Repeat the key noun instead of using a confusing pronoun.
- The thinking pause is 6–8 seconds in v2: long enough for a young child to look, think, and say an answer out loud, short enough to keep a 20-question episode moving. Put a gentle "Take your time!" at the start of the pause, then leave quiet.
- Never let narration, captions, music, or a sound effect give the answer away before the reveal.
- Music: light, cheerful instrumental — ukulele, soft piano, marimba, or xylophone — kept well under the narration, and ducked further during questions.
- Sound effects: a soft pop when a card appears, gentle ticking or a soft shaker during thinking, one warm chime at the reveal, a brief happy flourish after a correct answer. No alarms, sudden loud hits, or harsh ticking.
- Keep narration, music, and effects on separate tracks so timing can be fixed without regenerating video.
- Use only licensed, original, or public-domain audio, and keep a note of each source in the episode folder. Do not clone a real person's voice without permission.

## Quality gates — the lessons from the sample clip

The sample clip is a strong format reference — and it also shows exactly why gates exist. Two of its four labels are misspelled ("Sheap" and "Horsl"), and one card shows a cow while its label says a horse name. A child learning words from that card learns the wrong match. These are AI generation errors, not format errors — and every one of them is caught by a 30-second check before animation.

### Gate 1 — Script (before any generation)

- Exactly five questions in the batch; 20 in the full episode; no repeats within the episode.
- Each question has one, and only one, correct answer. Read the three wrong options again and confirm none of them also fits.
- Every fact sentence is checked against a reliable source, and the source is noted in the episode folder.

### Gate 2 — Card (before animating)

- Spell every word on the card out loud, letter by letter, including the question.
- Confirm each picture matches its label: the animal in the card is the animal the label names.
- Confirm the four pictures are clearly different from each other at a glance.
- Confirm there is no highlight, arrow, or emphasis on the correct card.

### Gate 3 — Final watch (before upload)

- Watch the entire export with sound on, start to finish.
- Azula and every Baker match the character sheet: faces, outfits, Azula's teal collar, no duplicates or extra limbs.
- Every thinking pause is really there, and no answer appears, is spoken, or is captioned before its reveal.
- Runtime lands in the 9–11 minute target for a full episode. If it is short, add a fact or a family beat — never stretch narration or pad with repeated filler.
- Captions are checked by eye, word for word.

## Publishing on PuppyToons-AI

Publish to [youtube.com/@PuppyToons-AI](https://www.youtube.com/@PuppyToons-AI). The old videos on the channel are being removed by Mo and play no part in this playbook — every episode under v2 is planned fresh.

### Title

Pattern: **[Topic] Quiz for Kids | Azula's Little Learning Quiz — PuppyToons**

Example: "Farm Animals Quiz for Kids | Azula's Little Learning Quiz — PuppyToons"

Keep the topic and "Quiz for Kids" in the title. Do not promise results, use misleading claims, or put an answer in the title.

### Thumbnail

- Azula large on one side, one friendly animal or object from the episode on the other.
- Three or four words maximum, in the same bold rounded style as the quiz cards — for example, "FARM ANIMALS QUIZ."
- Never show which answer is correct for any question in the thumbnail.

### Description

> Join Azula and the Baker Family for 20 [topic] questions made for children ages 3–8. Look at the four choices, take your time to think, say your answer out loud, then watch for the reveal. A short family scene with Azula, Maya, Sarah, David, and baby Leo appears between every round.
>
> Made for young learners and the grown-ups watching with them.

Add chapter timestamps for the intro, each round, and the recap once the final runtime is known. Credit any licensed music or sound sources the license requires.

### Settings and policy checks

- Set the audience designation accurately in YouTube Studio. These preschool quiz videos are directed to children, so expect to mark them "Made for Kids."
- Check YouTube's current guidance on child-directed content and on disclosing altered or synthetic content at upload time — AI-generated animation and voices are exactly the kind of material those settings cover, and the rules change.
- Turn on captions and check them manually after processing.
- Do not ask children for personal information, in the video, the description, or pinned comments.
- After publishing, watch the processed video once on the channel, check the thumbnail crop on a phone and a TV-sized view, and log the episode: topic, questions used, runtime, upload date, and anything to fix next time.

### Cadence

Consistency beats volume while the system is new. Publish the pilot round first, learn from making it, then settle into a repeatable full-episode rhythm — one finished, fully checked video is worth more than several rushed ones. Batch the writing (one session for a whole episode), batch the card generation (fives), and keep a question log so no question repeats across episodes.

## Worked pilot — Episode 001 v2, Round 1: Farm Animals

Build this first. Five cards, one intro, one family scene, a short recap — about 3 minutes assembled. If this plays well, Rounds 2–4 reuse the same system with new sub-themes (pets, jungle animals, sea animals, for a full "Animals" episode).

**Intro (family scene, ~25 sec):** Sunny garden. The whole Baker Family is present. Sarah holds a picnic basket, Maya holds up a drawing of a barn, David carries Leo in the carrier. Azula sits in front, tail wagging. Narrator: "Hi, little explorers! Azula and the Baker Family are visiting the farm today. Can you guess five farm animals with us? Look at the choices, think, and say your answer out loud. Ready? Let's go!"

### Question 1 — the sample question, corrected

- **Question:** Which sweet farm animal rolls in mud and says oink oink? *(11 words)*
- **A:** Fluffy Sheep — picture: one fluffy white sheep
- **B:** Little Piggy — picture: one smiling pink pig — **correct**
- **C:** Spotty Cow — picture: one black-and-white cow
- **D:** Cheerful Horse — picture: one friendly brown horse
- **Background:** sunny farm, red barns, green grass, flowers, butterflies
- **Fact after reveal:** "A pig's oink is how it talks to other pigs — and rolling in mud helps it stay cool."
- **Why this version exists:** it is the sample clip's question with its two label errors fixed and its cow card correctly labeled as a cow. Use it as the visual reference for layout, and as the standing example of why Gate 2 exists.

### Question 2

- **Question:** Which farm animal says moo and gives us milk?
- **A:** Gentle Cow — **correct** · **B:** Little Chick · **C:** Happy Goat · **D:** Farm Dog
- **Fact:** "Cows make a low sound that people write as moo."

### Question 3

- **Question:** Which tiny farm baby hatches from an egg?
- **A:** Little Lamb · **B:** Baby Chick — **correct** · **C:** Small Puppy · **D:** Tiny Kitten
- **Fact:** "A baby chicken is called a chick, and it hatches from an egg."

### Question 4

- **Question:** Which farm animal has soft wool on its body?
- **A:** Woolly Sheep — **correct** · **B:** Pink Pig · **C:** Brown Horse · **D:** White Duck
- **Fact:** "A sheep's wool is soft and thick, and it keeps the sheep warm."

### Question 5

- **Question:** Which farm animal says neigh and loves to gallop?
- **A:** Friendly Goat · **B:** Spotted Cow · **C:** Galloping Horse — **correct** · **D:** Little Donkey
- **Fact:** "A horse's call is called a neigh, and a fast horse run is a gallop."

**Family scene after Round 1 (~25 sec):** Azula + Maya at a farm fence. Maya holds up a drawing of a pig and giggles; Azula tilts her head, then lifts one paw in a paw-five. Narrator handoff: "Great guessing, explorers! Azula found some pets waiting at home. Ready for Round 2? Let's go!"

**Pilot recap (~30 sec):** the five correct cards return briefly, in order — pig, cow, chick, sheep, horse — narrator names each once, Azula reacts to each with one tail wag. End frame: Azula beside clean space for editor text, "Great thinking, little explorers!"

Fact-check note: the five fact sentences above are starting drafts in simple language. Verify each one against a reliable zoo, farm, or natural-history source at Gate 1 before the pilot is generated, and note the sources in the episode folder.

## Episode folder and naming

Keep one folder per episode, with the same structure every time:

- `script/` — episode package, answer key, fact sources, question log
- `references/` — Baker Family sheet and the approved sample card frame
- `cards/` — card images, named `R1-Q01` to `R4-Q20`, approved versions only
- `clips/` — animated cards and family clips, same numbering
- `audio/` — narration, music, effects, with license notes
- `export/` — final video and the completed Gate 3 checklist
